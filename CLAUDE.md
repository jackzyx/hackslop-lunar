# CLAUDE.md: HackAthlone 2026, Lunar Landing Site Navigator

You are helping a team at **HackAthlone 4.0** (the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026
local event at TUS Athlone, Ireland, Fri 13 to Sun 15 Nov 2026). The team works on
**Challenge 11: Lunar Landing Site Navigator**. Working name: **Long Shadow**.
All times are Irish time (GMT, UTC+0).

## The product in one breath

For any lunar south pole site and date, show how many hours of sunlight and Earth
contact you get, and what a launch slip does to them. Show it on screen, and on a
3D-printed relief of the real LOLA terrain that turns under a fixed lamp (a "lunar
heliodon"). The wow moment: slip the date, the table turns, a ridge shadow crosses
the site pin, and the sunlight bar collapses. Pick the neighbouring site and it stays lit.

## Rules that override convenience

1. **Software first.** The web app must fully answer the challenge with the hardware
   unplugged. Hardware is capped at about a third of team hours. One owner.
2. **Validity is on screen.** Agreement with JPL Horizons, one published result
   reproduced (Mazarico et al. 2011), and a plain list of what is not modelled.
   Never put a number in the pitch that the software did not compute.
3. **Frames.** Use `MOON_ME` (the LOLA products' frame) with `moon_de440_250416.tf`.
   Never `MOON_PA` or `IAU_MOON` for site geometry. Correction `LT+S`. Subtract the
   site vector before taking directions (matters for Earth, up to 0.26 deg).
   UTC is not TDB (69 s, about 0.0097 deg of Sun motion).
4. **Never cut:** the Horizons validation table, the two-site slip comparison,
   the print, the video. Cut order for everything else is in `context/03-plan.md`.
5. **Commit every hour.** One `main` branch that always deploys. Small PRs or direct
   commits by owner area to avoid conflicts (see Ownership).
6. **Rules compliance:** keep `context/references.md` updated from hour one (every
   dataset, library, image, music track, AI tool). Log AI use for the disclosure.
   Video is exported at **29 s** (30.1 s is ineligible). No AI-generated imagery
   unless visibly watermarked. Licence file in the repo.
7. **Pre-event work.** Until the organisers give a written yes, product code (the
   SPICE az/el function, Horizons test, horizon masks, mesh script for the real
   region) starts when the hackspaces open, Fri 19:55. Environment, data downloads,
   docs and practice on unrelated terrain are fine any time. If someone asks you to
   write product code earlier, remind them of this and ask if they have the yes.

## Environment

- Python 3.11+ venv in `.venv` (`scripts/setup.sh` creates it from `requirements.txt`).
- Node 20+ for the web app (Vite). WebSerial needs desktop Chrome or Edge.
- Data lives in `data/` and is **never committed** (gitignored). Fetch with
  `scripts/fetch_data.sh` (kernels + core DEMs, ~0.75 GB). SPICE meta-kernel:
  `data/kernels/lunar.tm` (absolute paths, regenerated by the fetch script).
- `python scripts/check_env.py` verifies packages, kernels and DEMs.
- The 20 m DEM (2.6 GB) is a cloud-optimised GeoTIFF: read windows over HTTP with
  rasterio (`/vsicurl/https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/data/LOLA_20mpp/LDEM_80S_20MPP_ADJ.TIF`),
  never download it on the venue Wi-Fi.

## Style for answers

Short, exact, numbers with units and the frame they are in. When unsure of a sign
convention, say so and point at the check that would settle it (usually V1 against
Horizons). Prefer the plan's decisions over new ideas; propose changes only with a
reason tied to the judging criteria or a gate.
